# TimeGym publication strategy

Updated 13 November 2026. The author chose a new manuscript for the redesigned package and, on 6 November 2026, to post it as version 2 of [arXiv:2105.01404](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.01404) rather than as a new submission. The replacement has not been posted yet, and the manuscript has not been submitted to a venue. 1.1.0 was published to PyPI on 5 November 2026.

The paper's central contribution is an executable verification workflow: specify forecast semantics, separate invalid outputs from capability gaps, evaluate conditional references or declared relations, and reproduce a failure before checking a known repair. The mathematical references, notebook-authored software, and controlled studies support that account. Synthetic generators, unit tests, proper scores, and metamorphic testing are established ingredients. Preserve the 2021 source as historical material and attribute each new component to the current version.

For arXiv, a new manuscript does not necessarily mean a new identifier. The present scope continues and corrects the original TimeGym work, so **a substantial replacement of [arXiv:2105.01404](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.01404v1) is the chosen route**. arXiv ordinarily versions corrections and expanded works under the same identifier, and may combine conceptually similar submissions even without substantial verbatim overlap. A separate, explicitly linked sequel is appropriate only if the final work is scientifically distinct; if the boundary remains ambiguous, ask arXiv support before submitting. This is an interpretation of the policy for this project, not an arXiv decision. [Official version policy](https://info.arxiv.org/help/versions.html), [submission guidance](https://info.arxiv.org/help/submit/index.html).

A replacement receives its own date and version number; earlier versions remain accessible, and the unversioned identifier displays the latest version. It does not make new experiments a 2021 contribution. Explain the corrected reference semantics, redesigned software, and new experiments in the replacement comments and manuscript. [Official replacement guidance](https://info.arxiv.org/help/replace.html), [version availability](https://info.arxiv.org/help/versions.html).

Keeping the 2021 identifier gives readers of the existing work continuity.

**Plan decided on 13 November 2026.** Version 2 is posted with the corrections and additions of that date: a capability research question, the statement of which results hold by construction, the exploratory answer-key alarm and the explanations of the random-walk and trend results (recorded under protocol 2.1 in `experiments/results-v2-followup/`), and the added related work. A study of generated mutants of the lag-Ridge code and a study of one nonlinear model, where the shift relations hold only approximately, are kept for the peer-reviewed version; that version also recasts the Limitations as threats to validity (construct, internal, external, conclusion). The target is an empirical software-engineering venue, with a submission of the package to the Journal of Open Source Software.

## Posting version 2

1. Pick the commit to post, run `python paper/build_standalone.py --check`, compile `paper/timegym.tex` as described in the [build guide](README.md), and record the commit hash.
2. On the arXiv user page, choose **Replace** for 2105.01404. Do not start a new submission.
3. Upload `paper/timegym.tex` alone. It contains the figures (TikZ) and the bibliography, so arXiv needs no other file. Check arXiv's compiled preview against the local PDF.
4. Metadata: title "TimeGym: What Can Your Forecaster Learn, and What Is Broken?" (without the `\newline`); the abstract pasted from the manuscript, which is plain text and must stay under arXiv's 1,920 characters; the same author; primary category cs.LG. A cs.SE cross-list fits the verification framing.
5. Comments: say what changed, for example "v2: rewritten for TimeGym 1.1 (https://github.com/diogoseca/timegym), with corrected reference semantics, redesigned software and two new controlled studies; v1 (2021) described the original prototype", followed by the page and figure counts.
6. License: keep CC BY 4.0, the license of version 1.
7. Once version 2 is announced, update `CITATION.cff` in one pull request: the reference takes the new title, year 2026 and the `v2` URL, and keeps the same DOI. Update the README sentence and the citation label in `tools/sync_citation.py` in the same pull request. The arXiv DOI has no version, so from announcement onwards it resolves to version 2 and must not stay paired with the 2021 title. [arXiv DOIs](https://info.arxiv.org/help/doi.html).
8. When a venue publishes the paper, add its DOI and journal reference to the arXiv record, which needs no new version, and cite the venue DOI from then on.

The strongest current framing is a methods-and-software paper with controlled diagnostic studies. More tests and more successful notebook runs improve the artifact; they do not establish external validity. A broader empirical claim would need independently selected faults from other implementations, a direct comparison with relevant framework tests, and documented repairs on additional real tasks. The existing AirPassengers example supports one known repair on one series. The selected implementation regressions and covariance conditions remain selected cases even when their evaluation seeds are new.

The completed supplement sharpens the argument: different declared relations miss different regressions, and restoring the specified recursive algorithm can worsen average forecasting error in a random-walk condition. The covariance study also exposes the loss of detection power alongside fewer clean suite alarms after multiplicity adjustment. These counterexamples make the verification claim more informative than a list of successful detections. Keep them prominent in the abstract and discussion; do not turn agreement with one reference implementation into a claim of predictive superiority.

Prepare a reviewable submission candidate by pinning an immutable source revision, reproducing both study protocols and the generated manuscript, recording its environment, and having the author review the argument, bibliography, figures, and code. Choose a venue for the resulting contribution and its evidence. A methods or research-software venue can assess the integrated workflow; broad claims about debugging effectiveness require the additional independent evaluation above. Venue selection, release, and submission remain separate author decisions.

The manuscript revises four earlier positions: treating a noise-free generator component as an optimal forecast without specifying the loss and available information; fresh sampling as a solution to multiple testing; synthetic success as evidence of general real-data reliability; and priority for forecasting testing itself. The closest reviewed related works are [Dwarakanath et al. (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1109/MET.2019.00014), [SynTSBench (NeurIPS 2025)](https://doi.org/10.52202/085713-4860), and [SimpleTimeBench (October 2026 preprint)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02058v1); for the financial scenarios only, [FinStressTS (KDD 2026)](https://doi.org/10.1145/3770855.3817578) and [FinTSB (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11704-026-51064-5). FinStressTS is a 2026 benchmark for comparing models and is not a predecessor of TimeGym, which dates from 2021. The univariate and multivariate financial process families, including jumps, hidden regimes, and CCC-GARCH, are software capabilities and illustrative reference demonstrations; they add no cases to the two recorded empirical studies. The [claim ledger](../paper/claims.md) links the specific evidence and exclusions; it is the check against expanding the abstract beyond the results.
